<p>Many HR document management software options are built primarily for storage and access.</p>



<p>The software can organize files, apply permissions, and allow document access when necessary. While these are all critical parts of contract lifecycle management, systems limited to this functionality address only the final step in the document management process.</p>



<p>In reality, <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/teams/hr/">HR teams</a> begin managing documents long before they need to be stored. However, these processes are regularly split across multiple systems, including the HRIS, email, <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/content-library-vs-shared-drive/">shared drives</a>, and eSignature tools. The constant handoffs create gaps in version control, audit trails, and visibility across HR processes that can lead to headaches when something goes wrong.</p>



<p>In this article, we’ll cover how HR teams actually manage documents, where common tools fall short, and how <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/teams/hr/">all-in-one solutions like PandaDoc</a> can offer document options that most systems fail to support.</p>

<li><strong>Document processes </strong><span style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px;"><strong>that use multiple tools</strong> become harder to maintain and can create gaps that </span>negatively impact document integrity.</li>



<li><strong>An all-in-one document solution like PandaDoc</strong> offers scalability, additional tools, and better long-term performance for HR teams.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The problem with generic document management tools for HR</h2>



<p>In many cases, <span style="margin: 0px; padding: 0px;">HR document management systems are built on</span> a folder-based model. Files are organized inside nested folders, which can only be accessed by those with specific permissions.</p>



<p>This approach works well for general file management, but it doesn’t reflect how HR document processes actually function.</p>



<p>Just like proposals, quotes, and other business documents, most HR documentation moves through a multi-stage workflow before documents are stored. Well-established workflows, such as hiring or onboarding processes, will also include some automation.</p>



<p><strong>See how you can <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/recipes/new-hire-onboarding-packets/">automate onboarding paperwork with PandaDoc</a>.</strong></p>



<p></p>



<p><strong>Here’s a quick example of what a standard hiring workflow might look like in a major organization:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>A hiring manager completes a final interview round, selects a candidate, and submits a request.</li>



<li>HR prepares an <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/offer-letter-template/">offer letter</a> using an approved template from their document library.</li>



<li>Compensation, role details, and start date are pulled from the HRIS or ATS (if connected) into the document.</li>



<li>The offer documentation is routed for internal approvals, usually across multiple stakeholders, including the hiring manager, finance, and legal.</li>



<li>Once approved, the document is sent to the candidate for eSignature. Typically, this is handled via an integrated, third-party solution like <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/alternatives/docusign/">Docusign</a>.</li>



<li>After the new hire completes the document, an HR rep stores it as part of a new employee record.</li>
</ul>



<p>Each step in the process depends on accurate employee data, defined routing, and clear ownership as a document passes through multiple stages.</p>



<p>However, in many HR environments, each step is actually handled across separate systems. Templates live in one tool. Approvals happen over email or via an internal messaging platform like Slack. <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/electronic-signature-software/">eSignatures</a> use a dedicated or integrated third-party solution. Finally, signed documents may need to be uploaded manually from an email attachment.</p>



<p>The split across multiple systems and platforms ultimately increases administrative overhead and makes it much harder to track and maintain documents as they move through the process.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">The documents HR actually manages (and what each one needs)</h2>



<p>Most HR teams are responsible for managing documents across onboarding, employee lifecycle changes, and offboarding.</p>



<p>Documentation such as offer letters, policy acknowledgments, NDAs, and others requires <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/creating-dynamic-document-templates/">defined and dynamic templates</a>, routing, and permissions. Managing these processes consistently is critical for maintaining accurate employee records and meeting HR compliance requirements.</p>



<p>Each document type introduces different requirements, all of which need to be supported by the same system.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Offer letters</h3>



<p>One of the most common documents, <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/pandadoc-offer-letters/">offer letters</a>, are regularly added to structured HR document workflows.</p>



<p>These documents rely on standardized templates, defined approval steps, and, almost always, require a legally binding e-signature from the prospective employee to validate the terms. </p>



<p><strong>Common documents in this category include:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Offer letters</li>



<li>Compensation summaries</li>



<li>Equity agreements</li>
</ul>



<p>Because these documents directly impact employee records and compensation, even small errors can create issues for HR systems.</p>



<p>The problem is that many generic HR systems don’t support these workflows at all. Everything from document creation to final eSignature is handled outside the HRIS, as HR professionals pass files among stakeholders, candidates, and technology solutions that facilitate negotiations and agreements.</p>



<p>If a candidate pushes back on specific elements of the offer, changes and updates can be difficult to track because those alterations aren’t captured in a unified <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/ask/what-is-an-audit-trail/">audit trail</a>. Instead, changes are handled piecemeal across multiple systems long before the document is finalized and uploaded to any HR software.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Onboarding documents</h3>



<p>When employee onboarding begins, document volume increases quickly, and consistency becomes much harder to maintain.</p>



<p>Multiple forms in the onboarding experience must be completed in a specific order, and many rely on existing employee data or are designed to capture additional information for employee records.</p>



<p><strong>Common documents in this category include:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Onboarding guidelines</li>



<li>New hire forms</li>



<li>Tax forms</li>



<li>Direct deposit forms</li>



<li>Employee information forms</li>
</ul>



<p>Many teams rely on workflow automation to handle this stage of the process. Documents are generated and sent with the information already on file, and new hires are intended to fill in the missing pieces to complete the hiring process.</p>



<p>However, because these documents support payroll, benefits, and compliance, missing or incomplete information can create downstream issues across HR operations and impact the overall employee experience.</p>



<p>Frustratingly, most generic document management systems don’t support onboarding workflows at scale. Instead, HR teams need to distribute and collect documents via email or <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/content-library-vs-shared-drive/">shared drives</a>, then manually update employee records. This creates gaps in audit trails, increases administrative overhead, and makes it harder for teams to maintain data accuracy.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Compliance forms and policy acknowledgments</h3>



<p>Documents dealing with compliance require consistency, visibility, and long-term reliability.</p>



<p>Organizations need to distribute these documents broadly, enforce access controls, and confirm completion across the entire workforce. While current compliance forms can be included with new-hire and onboarding paperwork, future updates need to be handled as separate processes.</p>



<p><strong>Common documents in this category include:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Standard compliance forms</li>



<li>Policy acknowledgments and updates</li>



<li>NDA and confidentiality agreement</li>



<li>Codes of conduct</li>



<li>Security and <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/how-to-write-a-privacy-policy/">data privacy policies</a></li>
</ul>



<p>Since documents in this category can have legal ramifications and may contain sensitive data, missing signatures or failing to track these documents can create legal risk.</p>



<p>Even though HR document management systems can store the completed files, many don’t support <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/features/send/bulk-send/">bulk distribution</a> or <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/contract-tracking/">real-time tracking</a>. HR departments end up managing these document processes manually, which increases the likelihood of gaps in compliance documentation and makes it easier to miss incomplete documents.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Employment contracts and amendments</h3>



<p>Any changes to employment terms need to be documented clearly and tracked over time.</p>



<p>Promotions, compensation or benefits updates, and role changes can all require updated agreements that reflect current terms while preserving historical context.</p>



<p><strong>Common documents in this category include:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li>Employee contracts</li>



<li>Compensation change agreements</li>



<li>Promotion letters</li>



<li>Role change documents</li>



<li>Scope change acknowledgments</li>



<li>Amendment agreements</li>
</ul>



<p>As employees move through their lifecycle, these documents reflect those changes and maintain a clear record for both HR and employee relations.</p>



<p>Unfortunately, many systems don’t support <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/features/collaborate/content-locking/">version control</a> at the level required for these workflows. Updates are handled manually, leading to inconsistencies in employee data and gaps in audit trails.</p>



<p>Without a unified system, document changes—including how they were changed and who approved those changes—become far more difficult to track.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Performance reviews and employee records</h3>



<p>Ongoing documentation also plays a key role in managing employee performance and maintaining accurate records.</p>



<p>These documents must be accessible to the right stakeholders while still protected by strict access controls. They also need to support long-term retention and allow for easy retrieval as part of employee records management.</p>



<p><strong>Common documents in this category include:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/employee-performance-review-template/">Performance reviews</a></li>



<li>Employee records</li>



<li>Disciplinary records</li>



<li>Feedback forms</li>



<li>Development plans</li>
</ul>



<p>Because these documents regularly include sensitive data, <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/features/report-and-track/document-repository/">secure document storage</a> and clear access controls are critical.</p>



<p>While generic document management solutions can store these files, they don’t support how performance-related documents are created, updated, or reviewed over time. HR teams are instead forced to turn to a much broader toolkit, which fragments document processes while limiting visibility.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Where the HRIS falls short</h2>



<p>HRIS platforms play a central role in managing employee records and maintaining structured employee information. They provide a reliable system for document storage, access controls, and core HR processes.</p>



<p>For completed HR files, these systems work well. Employee documents can be stored, sorted, and organized while providing easy access to authorized users. The problem is that none of these systems help to close gaps that appear earlier in the document management process. </p>



<p>HRIS solutions aren’t designed to support how documents are created, routed, approved, or signed. Instead, human resources teams are left to perform these tasks using external systems and third-party tools.</p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Document creation happens outside the system. </strong>Templates are usually stored separately and populated manually or via a system not connected to the HRIS. HR reps need to transcribe sensitive information manually, increasing the risk of errors and inconsistencies in employee data</li>



<li><strong>Routing and approvals aren’t structured.</strong> Without a formalized system, <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/workflow-approval-software/">approval processes</a> end up shoved into emails and messaging platforms. HR teams need to manage these processes manually, but even when handled efficiently, approval records may not be attached to the document.</li>



<li><strong>E-signatures are disconnected from employee records. </strong>To <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/electronic-signatures/">capture an electronic signature</a>, most teams need to upload a document to an external e-signature provider, download the completed version once the document is signed, and then manually upload the signed copy to the HRIS. Doing so breaks the audit trail and omits visibility into how a document was finalized.</li>



<li><strong>Audit trails are incomplete.</strong> Even though HRIS platforms will store the final document, they don’t capture the full history of changes, approvals, and document activity. This creates gaps in compliance documentation, forcing reps to chase approvals and update records across various systems when internal auditors need to verify specific details or workflows.</li>



<li><strong>Limited visibility into document status.</strong> HR teams can’t easily track where documents are in the workflow. Manual tracking and follow-ups are required to get documents moving when an approver or stakeholder fails to action important paperwork quickly.</li>
</ul>



<p>Over time, these shortfalls and gaps increase administrative overhead and make it harder to scale document workflows across the employee lifecycle.</p>



<p>Without a system that can connect document creation, routing, and storage, HR teams are forced to manage processes and handoffs manually, which limits efficiency and increases risk around sensitive information and HR compliance.</p>



<h2 class="wp-block-heading">What HR actually needs from a document management tool</h2>



<p>So far, we’ve covered the main issues that HR reps encounter while managing documents in their role. We’ve also covered where HRIS solutions fall short when assisting with this process.</p>



<p>The reality is that HR teams don’t need another system for document storage. They need a document management solution that supports how documents are actually managed within the organizational infrastructure.</p>



<p>A modern <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/blog/hr-document-management/">HR document management system</a> should support end-to-end document creation, oversee the process, and provide long-term storage options once a document is finalized.</p>



<p>Here’s what most HR teams actually need.</p>

<p>Without this feature, reps must manually enter employee information, increasing the risk of errors and inconsistencies. A consistency failure or a mix-up on the wrong document—such as a legally binding agreement—could create major legal problems down the line if a dispute arises.</p>



<p>To address this, teams need to connect their <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/integrations/for-hr-teams/">HRIS</a>, <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/integrations/crm/">CRM</a>, or similar tool with a document solution like PandaDoc. Doing so allows teams to connect document templates to existing data sources and pull that information across platforms.</p>



<p><strong>The process looks something like this:</strong></p>

<p><strong>3. Map the fields in your HRIS or CRM</strong> to match the variables in your PandaDoc document.</p>



<p><strong>4. Generate a new PandaDoc document from a template</strong> and wait while PandaDoc automatically inserts data from your information systems into the new document.</p>



<p>While the exact setup steps will vary depending on the software you use, this type of integration can massively streamline HR-based document generation.</p>



<p>Because all templates are managed in a single system (PandaDoc), teams can maintain consistency across employee documents while scaling document workflows more effectively.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Structured document routing and approval workflows</h3>



<p>Many HR documents require defined routing and approval steps before they can be finalized.</p>



<p>Unfortunately, most HRIS systems don’t offer a way to structure and automate these workflows. Instead, approvals are handled via email or messaging platforms, which limits visibility and creates gaps in audit trails.</p>



<p>All-in-one software solutions like PandaDoc provide <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/workflow-approval-software/">built-in automated routing and approval workflows</a>, ensuring that documents follow a pre-defined process. Routing can be configured based on role, document type, or internal policies.</p>



<p>Automating these approval flows allows teams to send documents without fear that something will slip through the cracks.</p>



<p><strong>For example:</strong></p>



<ul class="wp-block-list">
<li><strong>Teams can set up automatic routing in PandaDoc</strong> so that a document moves from one stakeholder to the next automatically as signatures are acquired.</li>



<li><strong>PandaDoc can also </strong><a href="https://support.pandadoc.com/en/articles/9714660-notification-emails-sent-to-document-participants"><strong>send automatic notifications</strong></a> to nudge stakeholders when processes have stalled.</li>
</ul>



<p>Using this approach ensures that all documents follow the same automated, standardized approval workflow. While this can be customized to various documents, the approval process itself is always automatic. Once the HR team starts the process, all they need to do is wait for the document to be completed before it returns to their inbox.</p>



<p>Plus, because systems like PandaDoc are cloud-based, documents can be accessed and signed from anywhere. Teams won’t need to handle paper files, download or scan docs, or switch systems during the approval process.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Built-in collaboration and redlining tools</h3>



<p>Some documents in the HR workflow require back-and-forth collaboration before they are finalized.</p>



<p>Offer letters, employment contracts, and policy documents may need edits, comments, or revisions based on internal feedback or candidate negotiations. But most HRIS platforms don’t track these changes because they happen elsewhere.</p>



<p>Without <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/features/collaborate/">built-in collaboration tools</a>, many of these changes are handled across email threads, attachments, or separate systems. By the time the document is entered into the HRIS, any changes or updates have been lost in the shuffle between communication and word-processing software like Microsoft Word or Google Docs, where they are made.</p>



<p>PandaDoc fixes this by including built-in collaboration and <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/features/collaborate/redlining/">redlining features</a> that let stakeholders review, comment, and edit documents within the same system. Changes are tracked in context, and version history is preserved as part of the document record.</p>



<p>Ultimately, this approach reduces reliance on disconnected tools and improves visibility into document changes. HR teams accessing a document can review all historical changes because each change is saved in the document history.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Integrated eSignatures with complete audit trails</h3>



<p>One of the biggest challenges with any HRIS system is maintaining an unbroken chain of custody for signed documents. Unless a document remains within a single system, some part of the creation, approval, or e-signing process will be missing when the document is finalized.</p>



<p>This isn’t the fault of any one system but a consequence of passing the document and customer information across multiple platforms. Each platform may retain a different portion of the audit trail, but no single system— including the HRIS—will have a complete picture of the document’s journey.</p>



<p>PandaDoc changes that by keeping everything inside a single platform. Electronic signatures are built into PandaDoc, so documents don’t need to be exported and re-uploaded to another solution to capture a signature. Instead, documents are created, sent, and signed in PandaDoc.</p>



<p>Because everything stays in one system, <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/features/report-and-track/audit-trail/">PandaDoc Audit Trails</a> capture everything from document creation all the way to final signature. This includes any changes or revisions to the document, enabling auditors and reviewers to better understand what happened after the original document was generated.</p>



<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Compliance-ready document storage with simplified access controls</h3>



<p>HR documents often contain sensitive information and data that must be managed in accordance with strict retention and compliance requirements.</p>



<p>Depending on company data policies, this may include maintaining audit trails, enforcing access controls, and storing documents securely over time. Assuming the company doesn’t require paper copies stored in filing cabinets, <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/workflow-approval-software/">paperless workflows</a> offer far more flexibility through things like nested folders and metadata.</p>



<p>With the exception of audit trails, this is one area where current HRIS solutions perform well. However, systems like PandaDoc can provide even better compliance by offering secure document storage, configuration permissions, and <a href="https://www.pandadoc.com/legal/security-practices/">data policies</a> that align with many HR compliance standards.</p>



<p>By centralizing these controls in a single document management system, HR teams can reduce risk and maintain consistent compliance across a vast collection of employee documents.</p>
